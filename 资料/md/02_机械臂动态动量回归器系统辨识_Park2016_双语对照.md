System Identiﬁcation Method for Robotic Manipulator based on Dynamic Momentum Regressor Kyongho Park and Youngjin Choi Abstract— The paper presents a momentum regressor using both numerical integration and Hamiltonian dynamic descrip- tion for robotic manipulator. It has an advantage in that the proposed momentum regressor does not require the acceleration estimation or sensor for the identiﬁcation of dynamic param- eters. In addition, the identiﬁcation algorithm is proposed by using Lagrange multiplier and bordered gramian matrix for the minimization of difference between the previous and the current estimates of dynamic parameters. The developed algorithm is intuitive and easy to implement in real-time fashion. Finally, the effectiveness of the proposed momentum regressor and identi- ﬁcation method is shown through the numerical simulations about a planar two-link robotic manipulator system. I. INTRODUCTION For precise control of robotic manipulators, exact knowl- edges about robotic motion description including its dynamic parameters such as link inertias, masses and friction coefﬁ- cients are required. For this purpose, an appropriate iden- tiﬁcation algorithm is also needed because various friction coefﬁcients are mainly varying according to the assembling and manufacturing procedures, even though the dynamic parameters are considered from the initial design phase. A variety of identiﬁcation schemes for linear and nonlinear systems have suggested in [1]. In addition, since the identiﬁ- cation on the dynamic parameters of robotic manipulator has been one of the classical research topics, several papers and textbooks have dealt with the topic [2], [3], [4], [5]. In the paper, novel identiﬁcation method is proposed by using new form of regressor, Lagrange multiplier and bordered gramian matrix through the minimization of difference between the previous and the current estimations of dynamic parameters. In the previous researches, the actual acceleration informa- tion was replaced into the desired acceleration one thanks to the adaptive control scheme. However, the paper does not contain the desired trajectory and the adaptive control scheme, but focuses only on the identiﬁcation itself. Since the identiﬁcation of dynamic parameters for robotic manipulator is basically based on the classical mechanics (equations of motion), let us review well-known three kinds of dynamic descriptions such as Newton-Euler equation, Euler-Lagrange equation, and the principle of virtual work. Actually, aforementioned three methods result in the minimal representation for the corresponding equation of motion if there are not non-holonomic con- straints. Generally, expressing the equations of motion with a minimal set of coordinates results in less differential equations, which are, however, more coupled and usually exhibit stronger nonlinearities compared to the non-minimal formulations [7]. The representative non-minimal formula- tion is the Hamiltonian equation of motion. Its advantage is that it brings a set of ﬁrst-order differential equations, though it results in the mixed form of differential equations. Actually the Hamiltonian equation of motion is a dual form of the Euler-Lagrange ones because the Legendre transform could be always found due to the positiveness of inertia matrix. In order to deﬁne a momentum regressor as new form, Hamiltonian equation of motion will be utilized for further mathematical derivations. The simulation and control of robotic manipulators de- mand the knowledge on the values of dynamic parameters. Computing such parameters from the design data of the mechanical structure is not simple, as an alternative, we may adopt CAD (computer aided design) tools which allow com- puting the values of dynamic parameters (links, actuators, and transmissions) on the basis of their geometry and the type of material employed. Nevertheless, the estimates ob- tained by using such techniques are inaccurate because of the simpliﬁcation typically introduced by geometric modeling. Moreover, the complex dynamic effects, such as a variety of joint frictions, may not be taken into account. Thus the robust identiﬁcation algorithm for the dynamic parameters is required. In order to identify the dynamic parameters of robot manipulator, ﬁrst, we establish the regressor from the corresponding equation of motion. In [2], the acceleration- based regressor was derived from the conventional Newton- Euler algorithm. In this paper, the momentum regressor is newly deﬁned by using the Hamiltonian equation motion, and it does not require the acceleration measurement or estimation for robotic manipulator. MOMENTUM REGRESSOR The robotic manipulator is generally described using Euler-Lagrange equation of motion as following form: τ = M(q) ¨q+C(q, ˙q) ˙q+g(q)+τf (q, ˙q)+JT(q)fext (1) where q = [q1,q2,··· ,qn]T is an n-dimensional conﬁguration (or joint displacement) vector, ˙q a joint velocity vector, ¨q a joint acceleration vector, M(q) an inertia matrix, C(q, ˙q) a Coriolis and centrifugal matrix, g(q) a gravity vector, τf (q, ˙q) a frictional torque vector, J(q) a manipulator Jacobian, fext an external force such as load, and τ a control input torque vector. The natural choice of a model for identiﬁcation of dynamic parameters is above Euler-Lagrange model of the manipulator, which has the property of linearity in dynamic parameters as following form: τ = YL(q, ˙q, ¨q)θ, (2) where YL(q, ˙q, ¨q) is a conventional regressor matrix con- taining all the nonlinear terms related to the displacement, velocity and acceleration, and θ is a constant dynamic parameter vector such as link inertia, link mass and po- sitions of center of mass, and friction coefﬁcients. The general method constructing the conventional regressor has been suggested in [2], [3]. It is referred to as a Lagrange regressor or conventional regressor. If the control input torque, displacement, velocity, acceleration vectors are all measurable every time instants, then it might be possible to identify the dynamic parameters θ of robotic manipulator, but the acceleration information should be provided for the algorithm implementation. Since the robotic manipulator system is not normally equipped with the accelerometer, we need to construct the estimator to make acceleration signal from the velocity or displacement through numerical differentiation. It might bring wrong identiﬁcation results. On the other hand, even though it has an accelerometer, it is not easy to combine different information obtained from two or more sensors because the measurement bandwidths of the sensors are different each other. Thus many previous simulations and experiments might bring inexact identiﬁcation results. As an alternative, new form of regressor that does not require the numerical differentiation or acceleration information is proposed in the following. Let us deﬁne the momentum vector as follow: p = M(q) ˙q, (3) where p is an n-dimensional momentum vector derived from the product of inertia matrix and joint velocity vector. Also the above is referred to as a Legendre transformation and it is always possible if and only if the inertia matrix is positive deﬁnite and symmetric. In addition, we know that the time derivative of the inertia matrix has the following relation with Coriolis and centrifugal matrix [3]: ˙M(q) = C(q, ˙q)+CT(q, ˙q), (4) where it can be interpreted that the time derivative of inertia matrix is equal to two times of even part of Coriolis and centrifugal matrix. After taking a time derivative of Eq. (3), if we rearrange it by using Eqs. (1) and (4), then we have ˙p = ˙M(q) ˙q+M ¨q =[C(q, ˙q)+CT(q, ˙q)] ˙q −[C(q, ˙q) ˙q+g(q)+τf (q, ˙q)+JT(q) fext −τ] =CT(q, ˙q) ˙q−g(q)−τf (q, ˙q)−JT(q)fext +τ, (5) where the above implies the Hamiltonian equation of motion that does not explicitly include an acceleration term. This is the reason why we utilize the Hamiltonian representation for equation of motion. Now let us deﬁne ﬁrst subregressor as ˙p = W1(q, ˙q)θ+τ, (6) where W1(q, ˙q)θ △= CT(q, ˙q) ˙q−g(q)−τf (q, ˙q)−JT(q)fext, (7) in which note that the ﬁrst subregressor is not dependent on the acceleration. Also second subregressor can be deﬁned from the momentum equation as: p = M(q) ˙q △= W2(q, ˙q)θ. (8) After integrating both sides of Eq. (6), if Eq. If above momentum regressor is utilized, then we know that it does not require the acceleration information but the numerical integration of the applied input torques and ﬁrst subregressor. The numerical integration is more stable and exact than the numerical differentiation in viewpoint of noise removal. As a matter of fact, Eq.
