Spatiotemporal Calibration of Doppler Velocity Logs for Underwater Robots Hongxu Zhao1, Guangyang Zeng2, Yunling Shao1, Tengfei Zhang1,Yuzhe Li3, and Junfeng Wu1 Abstract— Accurate fusion of Doppler Velocity Logs (DVLs) with other sensors requires both spatial extrinsics and temporal alignment. However, existing underwater DVL calibration methods often assume known lever arms and synchronized clocks, and rarely estimate full calibration parameters jointly. We propose Unified Iterative Calibration (UIC), a spatiotemporal calibration framework for a DVL and a generic co-sensor that can provide a continuous-time motion estimate. We model the base motion with a continuous-time Gaussian process, enabling velocity queries at time-shifted DVL timestamps. UIC computes a sequential initialization: (i) it estimates the clock offset and DVL scale using low- angular-rate segments via velocity-norm matching, and (ii) estimates the DVL-to-base rotation and lever arm through a covariance-corrected closed-form stacked regression with SO(3) projection. A nonlinear refinement further reduces residuals. Simulations with DVL outliers and pool experi- ments demonstrate robustness and high accuracy downstream odometry estimation. I. INTRODUCTION Doppler Velocity Logs (DVLs) are key sensors for underwater navigation, which can provide body-frame velocity measurements [1]–[5]. For multi-sensor fusion (e.g., DVL+camera/SINS), accurate calibration requires both the spatial extrinsics and temporal alignment, since even a small constant clock offset can cause systematic velocity mismatch under dynamic motion. Underwater DVL calibration has been studied mainly for DVL–camera systems and for standalone DVLs with navigation-grade reference velocity sources. Westman et al. [6] and Xu et al. [7] formulated DVL–camera calibration as trajectory alignment, but typically rely on known DVL–IMU extrinsics and do not estimate a lever arm and a constant time offset jointly. For IMU-free DVLs, works such as [8]–[11] estimate rotation and scale using SINS/GPS-type velocity references, which limits applicability when the co-sensor does not directly provide body-frame velocities. Temporal calibration for heteroge- neous sensors has been widely studied on land by fitting continuous-time trajectories and querying measurements at shifted timestamps (e.g., B-splines/GPs) [12]–[16], but has been less explored for DVLs. METHODOLOGY UIC consists of sequential initialization followed by local iterative refinement. The initializer estimates all components of Θ without manual or near-true guesses. The reference trajectory x(t) is inferred from the base measurements under the GP prior and is not jointly opti- mized with Θ, because a flexible trajectory could absorb spatiotemporal miscalibration and confound parameter estimation. Under the idealized EIV assumptions, the initializer is designed to enter a neighborhood of the true parameters, after which refinement further decreases the residual. A. Sequential Initialization Strategy Our initialization procedure separates reference mo- tion estimation from the calibration parameters dRb, dtb, κd, and bδT,d. Concretely, UIC first computes a reference trajectory estimate ˆXb and its uncertainty ΣXb from the base measurements ˚ Mb under the GP motion prior. Then, for any candidate clock offset bδT,d, the time- shifted motion states at the aligned DVL timestamps are obtained by GP interpolation, yielding ˆXd(bδT,d) and ΣXd(bδT,d). This stage does not use DVL measurements, so that the reference motion estimate remains indepen- dent of the unknown calibration parameters and admits explicit GP-based interpolation. According to [21], the GP regression/interpolation stage can be implemented eﬀiciently and provides both a reference motion estimate and an uncertainty characterization, which are useful for subsequent calibration. The large-sample analysis is derived under idealized modeling assumptions (e.g., correct GP regression/interpolation statistics and suf- ficiently excited motion), and is intended to justify the design of the initialization estimators rather than to serve as finite-sample performance guarantees. Directly optimizing Problem (6) from random initial- ization offers no strong convergence guarantees. Poor initial guesses often lead to divergence or convergence to suboptimal local minima. Let wRc(Tc) denote the camera orientation in the world frame at time Tc. Over a short interval [Tc,1, Tc,2] between two camera measurements, since the DVL has the same frequency as the camera, there exist two DVL measurements ˚vd,1 and ˚vd,2 at times Td,1 + cδT,d and Td,2 + cδT,d, which satisfy Td,1 + cδT,d ≤Tc,1 and Td,2 + cδT,d ∈[Tc,1, Tc,2].
